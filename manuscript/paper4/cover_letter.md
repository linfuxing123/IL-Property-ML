Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit the enclosed manuscript, "Graph neural networks and engineered descriptors trade on temperature in ionic-liquid property prediction," for consideration as an Article in *ACS Sustainable Chemistry & Engineering*.

Ionic-liquid electrolytes are central to sustainable energy storage, and their design depends on fast, reliable structure–property prediction across the enormous cation–anion design space. The field is split between descriptor-based QSPR and end-to-end graph neural networks (GNNs), with existing GNN studies validated by point-wise splits that are known to overstate generalization. Using a single 84,077-record dataset of 1,891 ion pairs and a group-disjoint (IL-disjoint) validation protocol, I show that a message-passing GNN trained end-to-end from molecular graphs beats engineered descriptors for the temperature-dependent properties—density (R² = 0.941 versus 0.926) and conductivity (0.751 versus 0.740)—while hand-crafted descriptors remain essential for the cold-start melting point (0.523 versus 0.399) and competitive for viscosity. The result is a data-regime rule for representation choice that is directly actionable for electrolyte screening.

This work is a companion to three related manuscripts that establish the data-density and feature-engineering levers on group-level transferability. It is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by another journal. All data, descriptors, and code are openly available on GitHub and Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21898948).

I am the sole and corresponding author. Thank you for your consideration.
