# Cover Letter

**Date:** 13 August 2026

**To:** The Editors, *Science*

**Subject:** Submission of Research Article — "Full-spectrum descriptors substitute for data density in ionic-liquid property prediction"

Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit the enclosed Research Article, "Full-spectrum descriptors substitute for data density in ionic-liquid property prediction," for consideration by *Science*.

The study asks a question of broad relevance to data-driven materials science: when predictive models fail on unseen chemistry, is the binding constraint data—or representation? My earlier work showed that per-compound data density dominates group-disjoint accuracy for ionic liquids, converting cold-start properties into predictable ones through a 7.7-fold data expansion. The present study performs the complementary controlled experiment: holding data, model, and validation fixed, it expands the feature set from ten hand-picked descriptors to the full per-ion RDKit set (458 features) and finds that group-disjoint R² improves for all four properties—most strongly exactly where data are scarcest, lifting cold-start viscosity from R² = 0.14 to 0.56 and conductivity in 2–3-sample groups from 0.08 to 0.81. The result is a quantitative statement that feature engineering and data acquisition are two tradeable levers on generalization, and it extends beyond ionic liquids to any molecular property prediction problem.

The analysis is fully reproducible: all data, descriptor tables, and scripts are open (GitHub; Zenodo), the evaluation uses IL-disjoint group splitting to avoid leakage, and the results are verified against the companion study's baselines.

As the sole author, I confirm that this work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has no competing interests.
